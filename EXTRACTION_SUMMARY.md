## Important Facts Extracted

### Lesson 3.1: Weeds (Estimated Duration: 5 hours)
- **Weed Science (3.1a):** Studies vegetation management in agricultural production systems, natural areas, and residential/urban properties; includes weed biology, taxonomy and ecology, biological control, cropping systems, integrated management systems, conservation tillage, transgenic crop tolerance, precision technology, and herbicides.
- **Definition of Weed:** Any plant growing where it is not wanted; plant that interferes with human activities; any plant growing out of place.
- **Role of Weeds (3.1b):** Compete with main crops for space, sunlight, water, and nutrients; potential habitat/hosts for insect pests and plant diseases; cause **~10% per year** crop yield and financial losses worldwide.
- **Crop Management System:** Technology comprising all planning, establishment, management, and postharvest to maximize profit and quality of market produce.
- **Historical Note:** **Jethru Tull** (Great Britain) was the first person to define weeds in **1731** in his book *"Hoeing Husbandry"*.
- **Concept of a Weed (3.1c):** Relative, ever-changing category; function of **time and place** (e.g., tall roadside weeds became dangerous with the invention of the automobile).
- **Characteristics (3.1d):** Rapid vegetative growth, profuse root production, early and efficient reproduction, survival in adverse conditions, propagules possess dormancy, induced dormancy, significant damage even at low densities, long-term survival of buried seeds.
- **Benefits vs. Harmful Effects:**
  - *Benefits:* Soil stabilization, wildlife habitat/feed, nectar for bees, aesthetic qualities, add organic matter, genetic reservoir, human consumption, employment opportunities.
  - *Harmful:* Compete for water/light/nutrients; unpalatable/poisonous in pastures; injuries such as lodging of **foxtails (*Alopecurus* species)** in horse mouths; lowered animal product value from sticky **tarweeds (*Madia* species)**.
  - *Disease & Pest Hosts:* **Powdery mildew** on prickly lettuce (*Lactuca scariola*) and sow thistle (*Sonchus* species); **Clubroot of cabbage** on wild mustards (*Brassica* species), saltbrush (*Atriplex* species), and Russian thistle; insect pest & invasive hosts including crabgrass (*Digitaria sanguinalis*) and *Parthenium hysterophorus*.
- **Classification (3.1e):**
  - *Life Cycle:* **Annuals** (<1 year; summer annuals vs. winter annuals; easy to control at seedling stage), **Biennials** (2 years; year 1 basal rosette + tap root, year 2 flower stalk + seed; e.g., Must thistle), **Perennials** (>2 years; most common in blueberry fields, most difficult to control; reproduce by seed [daisy], seed + roots [sheep sorrel], or vegetative means [bunchberry]; includes woody and herbaceous).
  - *Growth Characteristics:* **Broadleaf** (2 cotyledons, branching veins, distinct petals), **Grasses** (narrow upright parallel-veined leaves, jointed hollow circular stems at internodes), **Sedges** (perennial/rarely annual, wet poorly drained soils, triangular solid non-jointed stems).
- **Human Affairs (3.1f):** Asthma/respiratory/skin/poisonous weeds (**Parthenium**, **Ragweed**, **Rye grass**, **Privet**); drinking water supply dam weeds (**Water Hyacinth** *Eichhornia crassipes*, **Cabomba** *Cabomba caroliniana*).
- **Reproduction (3.1g):** Sexual (seed via egg + sperm/pollen; sole means for annuals/biennials) vs. Vegetative/asexual (stems, roots, leaves; **rhizomes** [underground stems], **stolons** [above-ground stems], **bulbs**, **corms**, **tubers**).
- **Crop-Weed Competition (3.1h) & Population Dynamics (3.1i):** Infestation defined by (1) number of species present, (2) density of each species, (3) distribution across the field. Environmental factors: soil temperature (primary influence on dormancy & germination), soil moisture, light signals, nitrates & soil pH, gaseous environment/oxygen (or anaerobic condition).
- **Dispersal & Management:** Dispersal via wind, flooding/irrigation, birds/animals, unclean farm tools/equipment, spreading animal manure. **Weed control** limits infestation for profitable crops; **Weed management** includes prevention, eradication, regulated control, restricting invasion, suppression, seed prevention, destruction. Diagram (p. 11) shows **4 Weed Control Methods**: Cultural, Physical, Chemical, Biological.

### Lesson 3.2: Arthropod and Vertebrate Pests
- **Entomology & Arthropods (3.2a):** Entomology studies insects and other arthropods (arachnids, myriapods, crustaceans). **Phylum Arthropoda** is the largest animal phylum (~**84%** of all known animal species).
- **Insects vs. Mites (3.2b):**
  - *Insects:* Class **Insecta / Hexapoda** (>24,000 species in Britain, >1 million globally); segmented bodies, jointed legs, exoskeletons; 3 regions: **head** (mouthparts, eyes, 1 pair antennae), **3-segmented thorax** (usually 3 pairs of legs -> "Hexapoda", 1–2 pairs of wings), **many-segmented abdomen** (digestive, excretory, reproductive organs). Anatomy diagram (Wigglesworth, 2023) labels compound eye, ocelli, labrum, antenna, mandible, maxilla, labium, spiracle, spermatheca, oviduct, rectum, cercus, ovipositor, ovary, nerve cord, femur, tibia, tarsus.
  - *Mites:* From Old English meaning "very small creature"; belong to **Chelicerata** (with scorpions, spiders, pseudoscorpions). Anatomy diagram (Walter & Proctor, 2013) shows **Gnathosoma / Capitulum** (hypostome, chelicerae, palpus) and **Idiosoma** (striations, genital/anal/sternal plates, setae) plus leg segments.
- **Insect Orders (3.2c):** 30-row chart (Gullan & Cranston, 2020) detailing order, examples, wings, mouthparts, and metamorphosis across Archaeognatha through Zoraptera.
- **Behavior (3.2d):** **Innate behavior** (genetically wired, e.g., moth dorsal light reaction) vs. **Learned behavior** (acquired through experience, e.g., honey bees learning environmental signals to locate food).
- **Importance (3.2e) & Management (3.2f):** 5 roles: Pollination & food production, Biological control of harmful insects, Animal feed, Recycling & waste clearance, Insect protein. Management: crop rotation, trap crops, crop diversity, inter-cropping, resistant/tolerant varieties, planting time, cultivation, mulching/sanitation, beneficial organisms, soil/water/fertility management.
- **Vertebrate Pests & Invasive Species (3.2g):** Subphylum **Chordata** (notochord replaced by backbone/spinal chord; cranium enclosing brain). Invasive species = non-indigenous organism causing economic/environmental harm.
  - *Major — Rodents (Order Rodentia):* Two constantly growing incisors kept short by gnawing. Five major species: *Rattus rattus complex* (Means) — common rice field rat; *Rattus argentiventer* — Asian rice field rat; *Rattus exulans* — Polynesian/bush rat; *Rattus norvegicus* (Berkenhont) — Norway rat; *Mus musculus castaneus* (Waterhouse) — Philippine house mouse.
  - *Minor — Bats, Monkeys, Birds, Philippine Wild Pig:* ~56 bat species in Philippines (out of 1,000 globally); smallest = **Philippine bamboo bat**; largest = **giant flying fox (*Acerodon jubatus*)** & **golden crown flying fox (*Pteropus vampyrus*)**. Monkey pest = **crab-eating Macaque (Raffles, 1821)**. Birds = **Class Aves**, Phylum Chordata (grain-eating pests). Wild pigs = **Visayan Warty Pig (*Sus cebifrons*)** & **Philippine Warty Pig (*Sus philippensis*)**.

### Lesson 3.3: Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
- **Definition & History:** Holistic evaluation and implementation of pest management strategies reducing pests to tolerable levels while maintaining environmental quality.
  - **18th–19th centuries:** Husbandry techniques (rotation, cultivation).
  - **1900s–1940s:** Chemical revolution (**DDT** [dichloro-diphenyl-trichloro-ethane], Organochlorides, Organophosphate, Carbamates).
  - **1962:** *Silent Spring* by **Rachel Carson**.
  - **1960s–1970s:** Plant protection concern; **1970s** term *IPM* first used in agriculture.
  - **Early 1980s:** Non-agricultural IPM literature by **H. and W. Olkowski** (founders of **Bio-Integral Resource Center – BIRC**), National Park Service manual, later museums.
- **Cardinal Axiom & Goals (A):** *"One pest control method will not be successful."* Four pillars diagram: Biological, Chemical, Cultural, Physical. Goal is **not** to eradicate pests, but maintain below economically relevant levels: (1) Increase farm profitability, (2) Improve environmental quality, (3) Improve public image of agriculture.
- **Thresholds & Economics:**
  - **Economic Threshold Level (ETL):** Pest density at which management action should be taken to prevent reaching EIL / economic damage (plotted below EIL on graph, p. 26).
  - **Economic Injury Level (EIL):** Smallest number of insects (injury) causing yield losses equal to management costs (damage exceeds control cost).
  - *Challenges:* Initial financial constraints, lack of expertise, inadequate research, lack of supportive government/policies, traditional farming.
  - *Advantages:* Lower cost intervention, environmental benefits, minimized pesticide residues.
- **Diagnosis & Field Factors (B–C):** Multi-cause problems are **"complexes"**. 8 implementation steps + 6-stage Basic Principles cycle (Prevent -> ID Pests -> Monitor -> Decide -> Control -> Evaluate). Field factors: weather (**temperature, day length, humidity**) and agricultural practice changes (new crops, field enlargement/aggregation, monocrops, plant density).
- **Five Control Methods (D):**
  1. **Cultural Method:** Resistant varieties, tillage, mulching, hand weeding/hoeing, pruning, trapping, handpicking, row covers, sticky bands, soil treatment, crop rotation, strip cropping, planting dates, trap plants.
  2. **Mechanical Method:** Manual/device removal or blocking access (pruning/raking, irrigation management, heating/steaming soils; images: hand picking, banding, wrapping, netting, trenching).
  3. **Biological Control:** Predators, parasitoids, pathogens. Chart (Walia & Bajaj, 2013): **Fungi (86%)**, **Bacteria (10%)**, **Mites (2%)**, plus predatory nematodes, protozoa, tardigrades, turbellarians, collembolans, predatory mites.
  4. **Chemical Control:** Natural or synthetic chemicals. **4 Color-Code Categories (p. 30 table):**
     - **Category 1 (Red label, POISON):** Extremely toxic | `1–50 mg/kg` | Monocrotophos, zinc phosphide, ethyl mercury acetate, etc.
     - **Category 2 (Yellow label, POISON):** Highly toxic | `51–500 mg/kg` | Endosulfan, carbaryl, quinalphos, etc.
     - **Category 3 (Blue label, DANGER):** Moderately toxic | `501–5000 mg/kg` | Malathion, thiram, glyphosate, etc.
     - **Category 4 (Green label, CAUTION):** Slightly toxic | `> 5000 mg/kg` | Mancozeb, oxyfluorfen, mosquito repellant oils/liquids, household insecticides.
  5. **Regulatory Practices:** Preventing entry/establishment of foreign pests; eradication, containment, suppression. Friendly Reminder: Identification of risk, Prevention of entry, [S]urvey and detection, Eradication, [R]etardation of spread, Mitigation of losses, Pest diagnostics and record keeping, Public information and education.
